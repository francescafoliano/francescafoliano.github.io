**Internal migration of young adults in England and social mobility**

The project studies how young adults in England move between areas, how economic opportunities shape those moves, and how moving relates to social mobility and spatial inequality. It uses Longitudinal Education Outcomes (LEO) administrative records. Young adulthood is the period of life when rates of residential mobility are highest.

![Out- and in-migration flows as a share of the young adult population at age 16, LEO 2014–2019](/images/adr_fig1.png)

*Figure 1. Out-migration and in-migration flows as a share of the young adult population at age 16. LEO data, 2014–2019.*

**Objective 1: descriptive study.** Map young adults' migration flows across local labour markets, comparing location at the end of secondary school with location in early adulthood. Outputs include an interactive website with breakdowns by socio-economic status and higher education status, and an examination of how flows changed during COVID-19.

**Objective 2: migration decisions.** Assess whether young adults respond to economic opportunities when deciding whether to leave their local labour market and where to go, and whether these responses differ by ability, higher education status and socio-demographic group. Such responses could reduce regional disparities, or deepen them through skill polarisation that leaves some areas behind.

**Objective 3: migration and social mobility.** Examine whether moving in early adulthood is linked to social mobility for individuals and regions: whether movers gain in earnings relative to similar non-movers, and whether mainly affluent young adults can afford to move, which could widen earnings gaps.

**Expected impact.** The findings are intended for academics and policymakers, and to inform debates on person-based versus place-based policies for local economic downturns, and on policies that encourage residential mobility.
